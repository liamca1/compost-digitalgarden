## Yield / Servings

4 servings

## Ingredients

### For the ginger–shiitake broth

- 25g dried shiitake mushrooms (1oz)
- 1.1L boiling water (4½ cups)
- 30g jasmine or other long-grain rice (1oz)
- 20ml neutral oil (1½ tbsp)
- 40g fresh ginger, thinly sliced (1½oz)
- 3 spring onions, white and green parts separated
- 2 garlic cloves, lightly crushed
- 15ml Shaoxing wine (1 tbsp)
- 20ml light soy sauce (1 tbsp + 1 tsp)
- 1–2g ground white pepper (½–1 tsp)
- Fine sea salt, to taste

### For the tofu and vegetables

- 500g medium-firm tofu (1lb 2oz)
- 2 medium kohlrabi, about 650g total (1lb 7oz)
- 150g bamboo shoots, drained if canned (5oz)
- 15ml neutral oil (1 tbsp)
- 5ml toasted sesame oil (1 tsp)

### Non-conventional twists, included in this version

- 1 unwaxed mandarin, zest removed in 3 wide strips with a vegetable peeler
- 120ml unsweetened pear juice (½ cup)
- 25ml unseasoned rice vinegar (1½ tbsp)
- 1 small pinch fine sea salt

### To finish

- 15g toasted sesame seeds (1 tbsp)
- 20g toasted hazelnuts, roughly chopped (¾oz)
- 10g coriander leaves or small shiso leaves (⅓oz), optional
- 1 spring onion green, finely sliced

## Method

1. **Soak the mushrooms.** Put the dried shiitake in a heatproof bowl, pour over the boiling water and soak for 15 minutes. Lift out the mushrooms, squeezing them over the bowl, then slice them finely. Strain the soaking liquid through a fine sieve lined with kitchen paper or muslin to remove grit.
    
2. **Make the pear seasoning.** Put the pear juice, rice vinegar and salt in a small saucepan. Boil briskly for 5–7 minutes, until reduced to roughly 45ml, or 3 tbsp. It should be gently sweet-sharp rather than syrupy. Set aside.
    
3. **Prepare the kohlrabi.** Peel the kohlrabi thoroughly, removing the fibrous layer beneath the skin. Cut one kohlrabi into 2cm wedges. Use a mandoline or vegetable peeler to shave the second into thin ribbons. Keep the wedges and ribbons separate.
    
4. **Toast the rice.** Heat a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the rice dry and toast for 3–4 minutes, stirring often, until it smells nutty and turns pale golden. Add 10ml neutral oil, the ginger, spring onion whites and garlic. Cook for 1 minute without allowing the garlic to brown.
    
5. **Build the broth.** Add the sliced shiitake and Shaoxing wine, then add the strained mushroom soaking liquid. Bring to a simmer and cook for 12 minutes. Add the soy sauce, ½ tsp white pepper and the mandarin zest strips. Simmer for a further 6–8 minutes.
    
6. **Cook the vegetables.** Strain the broth into a clean saucepan, pressing lightly on the solids. Discard the rice, ginger, garlic and spring onion, but retain the shiitake slices. Add the kohlrabi wedges, bamboo shoots and shiitake to the broth. Simmer for 6–8 minutes, until the wedges are tender at the edges but retain a little bite.
    
7. **Roast the tofu.** While the vegetables cook, pat the tofu dry and cut it into 3cm cubes. Heat the remaining 10ml neutral oil in a non-stick or well-seasoned frying pan over medium-high heat. Cook the tofu for 6–8 minutes, turning carefully, until two or three sides are golden. Season with a small pinch of salt.
    
8. **Finish the broth.** Add the kohlrabi ribbons to the broth and cook for 30–45 seconds only. Taste and adjust with salt and more white pepper if needed. Turn off the heat and add the toasted sesame oil.
    
9. **Plate.** Divide the roasted tofu, kohlrabi wedges, ribbons, bamboo shoots and mushrooms among four warm bowls. Ladle over the hot broth. Spoon 1–2 tsp of the pear seasoning around each bowl, rather than mixing it through the entire pot. Finish with sesame seeds, hazelnuts, spring onion greens and coriander or shiso.
    

## Serving suggestion

Serve immediately with steamed jasmine rice, or with a small bowl of plain millet congee for a more restorative meal. Keep the pear seasoning at the edge of the bowl, so diners encounter its bright fruit acidity intermittently rather than losing the clarity of the broth.

## Flavour notes

The broth is savoury and lightly smoky from toasted rice, with deep mushroom umami and the clean heat of ginger and white pepper. Kohlrabi contributes a crisp brassica sweetness, while roasted tofu brings browned, nutty depth. Mandarin zest gives a warm floral-citrus lift; the reduced pear seasoning adds a delicate sweet-sour finish without turning the dish into a fruit soup.

## Optional adjustment

If using aged dried mandarin peel, use a 4cm piece instead of fresh zest and add it with the broth aromatics. It has a darker, more resinous character than fresh mandarin and can become bitter if simmered for longer than 10 minutes.
